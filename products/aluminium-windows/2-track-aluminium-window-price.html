2-Track vs 3-Track vs 4-Track Sliding

2 track (this page)

Two parallel runners — typically glass + glass. Most economical premium sliding configuration per opening. Cannot add a third sliding mesh panel on the same frame.

3 track

Adds a dedicated mesh or third panel path — the most popular configuration in India for bedrooms and balconies. Budget Domal 3-track often beats 29mm 2-track on ₹/sqft; see 3-track sliding and Domal guide.

4 track

Four sliding panels for very wide openings — villa living rooms and full-width balcony walls. More aluminium, hardware, and machining mean higher aluminium window cost with glass. Details: 4-track price guide.

Sliding vs casement

Sliding saves interior swing space; casement swings for maximum vent on small wet-area openings. Full comparison: sliding vs casement.

Slim vs standard 29mm

Standard 29mm balances price and durability for most homes. Slim sightlines (38/40mm minimal) cost more per kg of profile — see slim luxury pricing for villa-grade openings.

Touch-type single-point lock included. Multi-point handle lock adds per-window charge. Rollers, end caps, and interlock gaskets are in the base band for 29mm system series.

Installation

2-Track Sliding vs Other Window Types

Which is cheaper on ₹/sqft?

27mm Domal 3-track systems often beat premium 29mm 2-track on raw ₹/sqft — but differ on glass stack, sealing, and hardware feel. Compare both calculators with the same glass spec before deciding.

Which is better for home?

Living rooms and balconies: sliding (2-track if no mesh needed, 3-track if mesh matters). Bathrooms and utility vents: casement or top-hung for compression sealing on small openings.

Maintenance

Clean tracks quarterly and check drain holes after monsoon. Casement hinges need lubrication less often than roller paths, but 29mm system sliding is designed for long-term daily use on mid-rise projects.

Best Rooms for 2-Track Sliding Windows

  • Bedrooms — glass + glass sliding when mesh is handled separately (fixed mesh or 3-track on the main balcony instead).
  • Apartment living rooms — medium openings where 29mm premium sealing and view matter more than mesh on every panel.
  • Internal partitions — glass sliders between dining and living without swing-space conflict.
  • Offices — DGU upgrade for HVAC efficiency on south-facing façades.
  • Villas (secondary openings) — pair with full elevation systems on feature walls; use 4-track on the widest spans.

Not ideal for: balconies where sliding mosquito mesh is mandatory — choose 3-track instead.

When Is a 2-Track Window the Right Choice?

A 2-track sliding window carries two shutters — typically glass + glass. It is the most economical sliding configuration and makes complete sense in the right places:

Best for: Bedrooms and small-to-medium openings where budget matters, internal partitions, and rooms where you don't need a separate sliding mesh shutter.

Honest limitation: With only two tracks, you cannot have glass + glass + a separately sliding mosquito mesh. If mesh is important for you (in most Indian homes it is), choose a 3-track window instead — that is exactly what the third track is for.

Series advice is the same as all sliding windows: 27mm Domal 2-track is cheapest but expect leakage and a 6 ft height limit. From 29mm system series the joints seal properly, hardware lasts, and high-rise use is no problem. Keep shutter width at least half of height for soft operation.

FAQs

What is the 2 track aluminium window price per sqft in India in 2026?
Premium 29mm series 2 track aluminium sliding windows typically fall between ₹1200 and ₹1400 per sq.ft installed, before GST, depending on glass upgrades, coating, mesh, and hardware. Budget 27mm Domal systems can start lower — see our 3 track page for Domal-style pricing.
Is a 2 track window cheaper than a 3 track or 4 track sliding window?
More tracks usually mean more aluminium, hardware, and machining — so multi-track systems often cost more per opening. 2 track is efficient for simple glass + glass sliding. 3–4 track adds mesh or extra panels for wider spans.
